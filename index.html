Case studiesWhat happened, and what I learned.

People Operations built from zero

Ridecell, Dec 2018 - Jan 2026
Challenge

On day one there was no documentation beyond outdated files, headcount lived in disconnected spreadsheets, and employee questions had no tracked channel. The workforce was 250-300 people across 3 entities.

What I did

Built a 40+ procedure SOP library with named owners and backup coverage. Launched ASKPeople on Jira and Confluence with tiered SLAs. Made Sapling the HR source of truth, integrated with ADP and connected through Zapier.

Result

The company grew from 3 to 5 countries on the same infrastructure. 6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits. Approval cycles dropped from 1-2 days to 2-3 hours, and 5-10 tickets a day were resolved in 24-48 hours.

What I would do differently

I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

One approval path for every HRIS change

Prezent AI, Feb 2026 - Apr 2026
Challenge

HRIS changes ran through an undocumented, unapproved process, which created real audit risk. Early feedback was that a form would be too process-heavy.

What I did

Built a Claude-powered Employee Action Form that requires documentation and approval before any HRIS change, plus Notion workflows for new hires and offboarding. Brought every stakeholder in and held firm.

Result

100% adoption within one to two months, an audit trail that had not existed, and preboarding that starts within 24 hours of offer acceptance.

What I would do differently

I'd have piloted the form with one team first. Some people said it was too process-heavy, and early proof from a small group might have won them over faster.

  • Claude
  • Notion
  • n8n
  • Rippling

A reorganization with cost visibility

Prezent AI, Feb 2026 - Apr 2026
Challenge

A reorganization affecting 18 employees across the US and India, and leadership had never seen the full cost of a reduction in force.

What I did

Built a cost model for total cost and per-employee severance and COBRA liability, and a severance calculator and drafter using Claude and Gemini that worked out each amount and drafted the agreement. Tracked every step on a shared sheet for Payroll and IT.

Result

Leadership saw reorganization cost for the first time. Agreement prep dropped from 20 minutes per employee to about 3 hours total.

What I would do differently

I'd have built the cost model before the reorganization was decided. Leaders could then have compared scenarios rather than just seeing the cost of the chosen one.

  • Claude
  • Gemini
  • Cost modeling
  • COBRA

30-60-90How I would start.

A proposed plan built around the responsibilities in the role description.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and map

  • Meet the SVP of People Operations and each leader. Learn what is working and what is held together by effort.
  • Map every People process, tool, and document, from onboarding to leave to compliance.
  • Learn how W-2 employees, contractors, and volunteers are tracked today.
Days 31 to 60

Design the first moves

  • Document the highest-volume processes as SOPs with named owners and backup coverage.
  • Set up one place for employee questions, with clear response times.
  • Build the compliance calendar and check worker classification and multi-state exposure.
Days 61 to 90

Ship and measure

  • Run the first review cycle or engagement program on the new foundation.
  • Support recruiting operations in Greenhouse during the next hiring push.
  • Report to the SVP on response times, onboarding completion, and open risks.
By month 6

The outcome

  • Processes are documented, reliable, and run without constant SVP oversight.
  • Escalations are clear: investigations, executive conflicts, and legal matters go to the SVP.
  • A calm rhythm for reviews, offsites, and compliance deadlines.

This is a starting point. I would adjust it after the first conversations with the SVP of People Operations and the leadership team.
